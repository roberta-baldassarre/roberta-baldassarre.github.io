Preparing for baby.

The notes I wish I’d had in one place: getting ready for a baby, finding your rhythm at home, and building a little support around you.

These notes began as a list for friends. Some are specific to San Francisco, others travel well. I tend to choose practicality over aesthetics: borrow where you can, try things before buying, and leave room to discover what works for your family.

This page collects practical highlights from my personal notes. For pregnancy, recovery, feeding and infant sleep, get guidance tailored to your family from your care team. Products, local services and availability can change.

Before baby arrives

Set up the nursery and the everyday stations while you still have energy. A few comfortable clothes, a manageable pregnancy pillow and supplies within reach made more difference to me than a perfectly styled room.

  • Try carriers in person: the one you can comfortably put on yourself is the one you’ll reach for.
  • Look into feeding support before delivery, so you know whom to call when you need help.
  • Choose a shared tracking app in advance if you’d like both parents to keep notes. We used Cubtale; Huckleberry is another option mentioned in my notes.
  • Make space in the freezer for easy meals and agree on practical jobs your support people can take on.

The hospital bag

Keep it simple and ask your hospital what it provides. I packed a few baby outfit sizes and an easy dress for the journey home.

  • Comfortable going-home clothes for you and baby, with an extra option.
  • Pyjamas or a gown, underwear and warm socks.
  • Toiletries, including what you want for a shower.
  • Snacks and drinks for you and your support person.
  • A baby blanket for the journey home and your infant car seat.
  • Your pump, if you plan to ask the hospital team for help using it.

Before leaving, ask the team which recovery supplies you can take home and what you’ll need next.

Coming home

Have a bathroom basket ready with the recovery supplies recommended by your team. I liked having everything together, instead of searching through cupboards.

Soups, stews and ready-to-eat snacks helped make meals easier. Ask someone to handle groceries, food and washing up. The most useful help is often very ordinary.

If you’re nursing or pumping, a little station with water, snacks, tissues, your supplies and a phone charger saves a lot of getting up. Show your support person where everything goes and ask your lactation team about fit, cleaning and milk storage.

Gear & registry

You don’t need everything at once. Borrowing helped us learn what we actually used; different bodies, homes and babies suit different things.

  • Getting around: I used an Ergobaby carrier and a Babyzen Yoyo2 travel stroller. Try folding, lifting and carrying a stroller before choosing.
  • Changing: a station at a comfortable height, with nappies, wipes, spare clothes and a bin close by. I found a changing table with wheels useful.
  • Everyday clothes: zip-up onesies were easier for us than rows of snaps. Add several sizes to your registry and warm layers for San Francisco.
  • Feeding setup: consider bottles, a bottle brush, a drying area and comfortable nursing or pumping clothes if useful to you.
  • Small comforts: bibs, a comfortable chair and an organised bag. I liked the idea of separate packing cubes for clothes, changing supplies and food.
  • Buy gradually: newborn clothes and nappy sizes can be outgrown quickly. Check return policies and avoid stocking too much before you know what fits.

San Francisco support

A few starting points from my notebook. Check current services, schedules, fees and suitability directly before booking.

  • Natural Resources — parent groups and a place to look for community.
  • Bay Area Babywearing — help exploring carriers and fit.
  • Golden Gate Baby — postpartum doula support.
  • Jen Kiatta — lactation support listed in my notes.
  • Kitchen Doula — prepared food for the postpartum weeks.
  • NOPNA calendar — neighbourhood events and opportunities to meet other parents.

Library storytimes were an easy reason to get out of the house. We loved the one in Bernal Heights. Parent groups and neighbourhood walks can also help the early weeks feel less isolated.

Little outings & travel

Start with somewhere close, pack a change of clothes and leave room to head home early. My everyday bag had separate places for changing supplies, food and spare layers.

Before a bigger trip, check your airline’s current stroller and car-seat policies, the destination’s child-restraint requirements and the official passport requirements for your child’s citizenship. Leave time for appointments and documents.
